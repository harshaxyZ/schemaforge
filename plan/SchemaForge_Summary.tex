\section*{What it is}
SchemaForge turns a plain-English migration request into an evidence-backed decision. TrueForge
drives the loop: the agent inspects the live PostgreSQL target, maps dependencies, synthesises
forward and rollback SQL, rehearses it against a copy of real data, and produces a decision packet.
Then it stops. Production is only changed after a human signs an approval bound to that exact
migration.

\section*{The boundary}
Write credentials never sit in the agent loop. Two MCP servers run with different secrets:
\textbf{sf-core} holds read-only and shadow access; \textbf{sf-executor} holds the single
production write role and the approval key. A separate terminal tool signs approvals. The
executor re-checks the signature, the SQL hash, the schema fingerprint, and a single-use
ledger before it applies anything. Forged, edited, expired, replayed, or stale approvals are
each rejected with their own code.

\section*{Demo}
Two requests, five minutes. Making \texttt{users.email} \texttt{NOT NULL} finds fourteen null
rows in rehearsal and returns \textbf{DO NOT APPLY} with production untouched. Adding a nullable
\texttt{archived\_at} column passes every check, freezes at the gate, and applies only after the
human types the migration hash. Post-apply verification confirms the result. The refusal matters
as much as the success.

\section*{Done when}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Both scenarios run live against real PostgreSQL, no hand-written SQL.
  \item The refusal is produced by rule code, not by model opinion.
  \item Every rejection path has a passing automated test.
  \item A stranger can clone the repository and reproduce the run.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Where it stops}
Six ways a production apply is refused. Each has its own rejection code and its own test.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{6.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Attempt} & \textbf{Code} & \textbf{Caught by} \\
\midrule
Token signed with the wrong key & \texttt{BAD\_SIGNATURE}  & HMAC verify in the executor \\
SQL edited after approval       & \texttt{HASH\_MISMATCH}  & Recomputed hash of the SQL \\
Approval reused                 & \texttt{REPLAYED}        & Single-use ledger in the database \\
Approval too old                & \texttt{EXPIRED}         & Short time-to-live on the payload \\
Schema changed since rehearsal  & \texttt{SCHEMA\_DRIFT}   & Fingerprint recaptured before apply \\
Prohibited statement            & \texttt{PROHIBITED}      & Parser, plus a restricted write role \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section*{How the verdict is decided}
Fixed rules, first match wins. The model explains the verdict; it cannot change it.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{4.1cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Evidence} & \textbf{Verdict} \\
\midrule
Prohibited or unparseable statement      & DO NOT APPLY \\
Rehearsal failed, or violations found    & DO NOT APPLY \\
Invariant check failed after migration   & DO NOT APPLY \\
Schema drifted, or rollback unproven     & REVIEW \\
Application references flagged           & REVIEW \\
Everything green                         & APPLY \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section*{Stack}
TrueForge harness, two MCP servers in TypeScript, PostgreSQL 16 in Docker Compose, a terminal
approval tool. No mocked responses: the databases, roles, rehearsal and migration are real.

\section*{Not building}
Multi-database support, Kubernetes, chat integrations, a login system, a web UI, multi-agent
orchestration, or automatic rollback of changes that cannot be reversed.
